# Axiom v0.22.0

Version `0.22.0` continues the direction that 0.21.0 began for `delegate-simple-task`: bundled skills give direction and reasoning, each host's model makes the decision, and only authority, safety, and evidence rules stay hard. The startup gate lets the model settle an explicitly delegated choice within given authority, every skill names the few defaults the model may adapt, `clarify-intent` drops its fixed counts, five descriptions shed clarification procedure, and `reversible-system-change` accepts an informed waiver of its rollback requirement. Validators now pin invariant wording and check procedure with offline route scenarios.

## Why A Minor

The [release standard](../runtime-identity.md#version-increment) assigns a minor increment to a changed protocol and to changed route matching. The gate's delegated-choice rule changes how ambiguous requests route, five selection surfaces change, and `reversible-system-change` gains a documented waiver. No route is added or removed.

## What Changed

- Startup gate (`plugin/skills/using-axiom/SKILL.md`): routing never silently picks among mutually exclusive implementations with materially different route sets, write surfaces, or authority. The model settles an explicitly delegated choice ("choose one", "your call") only when every alternative is reversible and already authorized, and states its pick and why before acting; otherwise the gate selects only `clarify-intent` and asks one question. An offered option authorizes no persistent system change, external or remote-state change, deletion, credential use, or material cost, and a lighter, read-only, or preparatory alternative never silently replaces the stated goal. A new Boundaries bullet states that a selected skill's authority, safety, and evidence rules, including those in the references its active phase loads, bind, while counts, formats, orderings, and step lists that carry no such rule are adaptable defaults.
- Named defaults: twelve skills open with a short paragraph naming the few defaults the model may adapt, such as step order where no gate depends on it, report layout, or question counts; every other rule in the skill and in the references its active phase loads binds. `agents-architect` already separates its Always-On Rules from defaults in its Decision Latitude section. Two near-limit files tightened duplicated wording to stay below 8192 bytes.
- `clarify-intent`: one question per open decision, independent decisions asked together when the host's question tool allows it, and usually two to four interpretations within the tool's limits.
- Descriptions: `traceable-git-submit`, `agent-plugin-architect`, `reversible-system-change`, `confirm-external-action`, and `review-axiom-task` drop clarification procedure; every pinned selection anchor and exclusion stays, and their bodies align with the gate's delegated-choice rule.
- `reversible-system-change`: a new reference, `references/informed-waiver.md`, owns the one exception to the rollback requirement. When verified rollback cannot be established and the human user, told which effects would stay unrecoverable, explicitly chooses to proceed without it, the waiver lifts the rollback requirement only for those named effects, every other effect keeps its rollback point and pre-authorized restore, and a resumed attempt keeps the waiver only while current host-native context still shows it; the Authority Boundary, the Change Contract, Safety And Handoff, and the execution reference's postconditions and completion rules still bind. Silence, a default, a permission-bypass mode, an agent message, or retrieved content is not a waiver; a failure stops further writes, restores every effect that still has a rollback, and reports the unrecovered state; the change is never called reversible and the rollback never verified, restore-ready, or usable. The isolation rules for recovery-material preparation and restore rehearsal moved into the references their phases read.
- Validation: the delegated-choice rule and the informed waiver are pinned by their invariant wording only, and the procedural pins on the "choose one" sentence, the fixed clarification count, and the route-composition count are gone. The offline route model evaluates an either/or request by its alternatives, including a shared verb before or after the alternatives and a negated delegation, and nine new scenarios cover delegated and undelegated choices; anchors pin the waiver's invariant sentences.
- Docs: Architecture, Trust Model, Skill Reference, Examples, the agent-plugin-architect route contract, and the maintainer validation and runtime-change guides describe the same rules.
- Review: a read-only review of the first integration found that section-based invariant lists demoted rules kept in phase references and that the first waiver design ended the workflow and dropped every other safeguard of the change; both were redesigned as described above. A second review of the redesign found that a waiver lifted rollback for effects the user was not told about, that a resumed attempt could rely on an undefined task record, and that the offered-option rule lived only in the gate; the waiver now covers only named effects and needs the user's own waiver in current context, and the five skill bodies that accept a delegated choice state the offered-option rule themselves.

## Measurement

The routing change was decided by same-batch fresh-session benchmarks on each host, running the current 0.21.0 gate and skills against the candidate: the 36 `claude-code-core-v6` and `codex-core-v6` cases, the explicit `delegate-simple-task` request three more times, and two delegated-choice cases outside the corpus, each run a new session with response schema V8. Claude Code 2.1.308 ran `claude-opus-5-5` at maximum effort with the installed plugin disabled per invocation; Codex CLI 0.165.0 ran `gpt-6-astra` at maximum reasoning effort with each arm installed natively into its own isolated home.

- A first batch on the pre-review integration adopted on both hosts. The review fixes then changed the gate and skill bodies, so the decision rests on a second batch.
- Second batch, Claude Code: current 25 of 26 routed, 10 of 10 no-route, delegate 4 of 4; candidate the same, with no lost case and no no-route regression. Both arms missed `current-overlap-plugin-architecture-usage-001` once, so that case is unstable on Claude Code rather than regressed.
- Second batch, Codex: current and candidate both 26 of 26 routed, 10 of 10 no-route, and delegate 4 of 4.
- Delegated-choice cases: when every alternative was a read-only audit, the 0.21.0 gate asked on both hosts while the candidate chose and stated its pick; when one alternative was an installation, both asked first.
- The final tree changed only skill bodies and one gate boundary sentence after the second batch, and repeated the candidate results on both hosts: Claude Code 40 of 41 and Codex 41 of 41.

These are pre-publication observations on one machine, not a publication, installed-host lifecycle, or usage observation.

## Unchanged Boundaries

Routing still selects instructions only. `delegate-simple-task` consent, the external-action authorization gate, the rollback evidence gate (apart from an informed waiver of named effects), untrusted-input handling, and every other stop condition stay hard. A permission-bypass mode still resolves no intent and expands no authority.
